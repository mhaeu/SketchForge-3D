# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **A start view of your own:** New designs always opened looking from the front right, and Home went back there. Settings → Appearance now has **Start view** with **Use current view**, which keeps the angle, pan and zoom on screen, and **Standard**, which goes back to the usual view. New designs open in it, and Home (and `sketchforgeCaptureView("home")`) goes back to it. It is kept per browser (`sketchforge.editor.startView`), as the perspective camera sees it: a flat view's zoom becomes the distance that shows as much (`perspectiveDistanceForFlatView`), so it looks the same in either projection. Unit tests in `savedViews.test.ts` for storing and forgetting it, broken entries and that conversion (the standard view, 194.5 mm away at 38°, comes back at the same distance, and at half the distance with zoom 2). Not tried on screen. Not taken over: the MCP action. From layerling 1.109.0 (#853).
- **Round bodies are shaded three times faster:** Threads, springs, gears, lofts, rounded boxes and honeycombs got their smooth and sharp edges from three's `toCreasedNormals`, which builds a text key for every corner twice. They now use layerling's faster version (`lib/creasedNormals.ts`, which finds each corner once per vertex), which gives the same normals. Measured on a 20 x 60 mm thread of 27,800 triangles, median of five runs: 86.0 ms before, 28.4 ms now, largest difference in a normal 4.6e-18. Unit tests in `creasedNormals.test.ts` compare the two on that thread and on a sphere at a sharp and a smooth crease angle. From layerling 1.118.0.
- **Align in the sketch:** Two circles for a ring could only be centred on each other by hand. With two or more outlines selected, the sketch's action bar now has Align left, Centre left and right, Align right, Align top, Centre top and bottom and Align bottom. An outline is the selected points that lines join; each moves as a whole, handles with it, and they line up on the frame round all of them, in one undo step. `lib/sketchAlign.ts` and its four unit tests are layerling's; SketchForge has no locked sketch points, so a lock only counts when a point brings one. Not tried in the browser. Not taken over from the same release line: the mirror that adds a copy across an axis or a line (SketchForge's sketch mirror flips the selection in place). From layerling 1.108.0 (#836).
- **Curve through points:** With three or more points of an outline selected, the sketch's action bar now has **Curve through points** (other programs call it "Fit curve"): it lays one flowing curve from the first selected point to the last, through every point in between, with the handles the Smooth curve tool gives (a sixth of the way between each point's neighbours). The sides beyond the two ends stay as they are; on a closed outline the curve takes the short way round the selected points, and with every point selected the whole outline turns round. `lib/sketchCurveThroughPoints.ts` and its seven unit tests are layerling's, contributed there by @gogades, on SketchForge's own path order (`orderedCadSketchPaths` without construction lines). Not tried in the browser. From layerling 1.110.0.
- **Three-point arc in the sketch:** A new drawing tool, **Arc**, next to Line: click the two ends, then a point the arc runs through, and a true circular arc is laid between them, with its radius in the message. After the second click the chord stands as a straight segment, which the third click bends; an end on an existing point joins it, so an arc can close an outline. It is the sketch's own arc between two points (`bulge`), so it can still be bent by its apex afterwards; the bulge is at most four times the chord, so an arc of nearly a full circle is flattened to that. The tool's icon is layerling's. Unit tests in `sketchArcThreePoints.test.ts` (`arcThroughThreePoints` in `sketchArcs.ts`): a half circle through its top, an arc of r 5 through a point that is not its apex (bulge 2), the long way round (bulge -8) and three points on a line. Not tried in the browser; there is no live preview of the arc before the third click. From layerling 1.94.0 (#643).
- **Alt and the lift arrow makes a copy:** Dragging the lift arrow above a shape with Alt held from the start now leaves the shape where it is and makes a copy where you let go, so copies can be stacked straight up, as Alt and dragging the shape already did sideways. While you drag, the shape stands in for its copy; on release it goes back and the copy is made by the same path as the sideways copy (`duplicate` in `TransformDragState`, `duplicateShapesMoved` now takes the shapes as they were). The height handle makes no copy. Not tried in the browser. From layerling 1.110.0 (#865).
- **Select other: reach a shape behind another:** A hole inside a body, or a part behind another, could only be clicked after turning the view, or picked in the object list. A right click without dragging, where more than one shape lies under the pointer, now opens a short list of every shape the ray passes through, front to back, each once, by the same rules as a click (in front of the section plane, no reference image); a click on a name selects it, Shift adds it, Esc or a click elsewhere closes the list. Dragging with the right button still pans the view. SketchForge has no right-click menu, so the list is its own; the outline through what stands in front, which layerling draws for the row under the pointer, is not taken over. Not tried in the browser. From layerling 1.108.0 (#804).
- **Four saved views per design:** Shift+7, Shift+8, Shift+9 or Shift+0 saves the view exactly as it is (angle, pan, zoom, and flat or perspective) under that key, and 7, 8, 9 or 0 alone brings it back; a message confirms the save, and an empty key says how to fill it. The key's position counts, not its sign, so it works on a German keyboard, where Shift turns 7 into a slash and 0 into the equals sign that would otherwise zoom, and on AZERTY; the number pad works too. The views are kept in this browser per design, next to its workspace settings, and are not part of the undo history (`lib/savedViews.ts`). Unit tests in `savedViews.test.ts` for the keys, broken entries and the storage per design; not tried on screen. Not taken over: the `.lyl` field and the MCP action. From layerling 1.97.0 (#657).
- **Cookie cutter:** Combine has a new tool, **Cookie cutter**: the outline the selection stands on (the cut 0.05 mm above its underside, of any shape, text, sketch or imported mesh) becomes a thin wall, 15 mm high, whose top runs in at 45 degrees to a 0.4 mm cutting edge, and a flat rim round its foot to press on, as one mesh body in place of the selection. The outline is mirrored, as the cutter is printed on its rim and turned over to cut, so a text comes out the right way round. Holes in the outline - the inside of an e - get walls of their own, held to the outer wall by bridges at the rim. The panel sets the wall, height, cutting edge, rim width and height, mirroring and the bridges' count, width and height. Built here with Manifold (`lib/cookieCutter.ts`): in layerling the wall and rim are fill modes of a sketch and stay editable one by one, and those fill modes are not in SketchForge, so the cutter is a mesh and the panel's values are set before it is made. Unit tests in `cookieCutter.test.ts`: a 40 mm square gives 50.4 mm across, 15 high, genus 1; the cutting edge takes material off; a square ring is two loose parts without bridges and one with them; an L-shaped body at x 15, z 10 and y 5 gives a cutter standing at y 5 to 20 round the same place, with its foot mirrored to x 35 to 45. Not taken over: the plate instead of bridges, the lower imprint walls, the fillets, curved text outlines (the outline comes from the mesh), and the MCP action. Not tried in the browser. From layerling 1.118.0 to 1.121.0 (#982).
- **Gridfinity bins, baseplates and trays:** A new shape in the library, **Gridfinity**, builds the open storage system's parts from its published measurements: bins on the 42 mm grid, 0.5 mm smaller than their cells, 7 mm per height unit and 4.4 mm for the stacking lip, with the stepped feet under every cell; baseplates of 42 mm per unit, 5 mm tall, with a socket in every cell; and trays of any size without a grid. A dragged box snaps back to whole units. The panel offers the type, the units across, deep and high, the stacking lip, magnet holes (6.5 mm, 2.4 deep), the half-size grid of 21 mm, the wall thickness, compartments across and deep, a finger scoop at the front and a label shelf at the back. The geometry is layerling's `gridfinityGeometry.ts` taken over whole, so the settings it has beyond these (shelves and scoops on every wall, open sides, padding to fit a drawer, corner radii, a lip chamfer, divider positions, labels in each compartment) are kept in a design and drawn, only not offered in the panel yet; dividers, shelves and scoops are joined by Manifold, which now loads as soon as a Gridfinity shape is in the design. Unit tests in `gridfinity.test.ts`: a new bin is 2 x 1 x 3 units, 83.5 x 41.5 x 25.4 mm, and its mesh is one closed body of genus 0; a box dragged to 100 x 70 x 40 snaps to 83.5 x 83.5 x 39.4; a 3 x 2 baseplate is 126 x 84 x 5 with six sockets through it; the magnet holes take away 630 to 640 mm3 (eight of 637.1 in round); dividers and a label shelf add volume; the settings survive a `.skf` file. Not taken over: the exact body for the edge tools (here they work on the mesh), the MCP settings and the guide chapter. Not tried in the browser. From layerling 1.84.0 (#381) to 1.116.1.
- **Offset, and Cut out with a clearance:** A new tool **Offset** next to Hollow grows or shrinks one body along its faces by 0.01 to 10 mm, with round or sharp outer corners - the clearance of a press fit or a snap joint, where scaling would move the holes and pegs and Hollow only goes inward. It is the kernel's offset that Hollow already uses inward (`offsetSolid` in `cadOffset.ts`), run through the same session as Hollow, so the body has to be one the kernel can take. With **Keep the original and add the offset body as a hole** the part stays and a grown copy is added as a hole. **Cut out** in the Combine group takes two selected solids, keeps the smaller one where it is and cuts a copy of it, grown by the clearance, out of the other; a button swaps the two, and the result is an ordinary group. Measured with the real kernel in `cadOffset.e2e.ts`: a 40 x 30 x 20 box grown by 0.2 is 40.4 x 30.4 x 20.4 with sharp corners; grown by 2 it is 44 x 34 x 24 sharp and 35564.5 mm3 round, which is the box plus six slabs, twelve quarter cylinders and eight sphere eighths; shrunk by 1 it is 38 x 28 x 18; a plate with a 5 mm hole grown by 0.5 is 41 x 31 x 21 less a hole of r 4.5; a shrink of 5 on a 4 mm cube is refused. Not taken over: the offset as an undoable entry in the edge list (here it is baked, as Hollow is), a clearance of 0 in Cut out (the smallest is 0.01 mm), and the MCP actions. The panels were not tried in the browser. From layerling 1.113.0 (#919, #927).
- **Hull: shapes wrapped in one convex body:** Combine has a new tool next to the Intersection, **Hull**: it wraps the selected shapes in their convex hull, the smallest body without dents round all of them, as if shrink-wrapped. Two spheres become a capsule, four corner posts a box with round corners, a half sphere on a cylinder a dome without a step. Holes in the selection are cut out of the hull afterwards. The parts stay inside (`groupOperation: "hull"`, kept in `.skf` as a group with the operation in its definition): Edit group opens it and Done wraps the parts anew, Ungroup gives them back, and a later group, cut or the edge tools take the hull as its mesh rather than its parts. The hull is a mesh from `Manifold.hull`, so round shapes show facets. `sketchforge_hull_objects` does the same over MCP. Unit tests in `hull.test.ts` check the calculation (two spheres of r 5, 20 apart: within 1 % of the capsule's 2094.4 mm3; four posts of r 2 on a 20 mm square: 24 x 24 mm with the corners rounded, 5726.3 mm3; a 4 x 4 hole through the hull of two cubes: 3000 less 160 mm3, genus 1) and the `.skf` round trip; the button and Edit group were not tried in the browser. From layerling 1.110.0 (#863).
- **Designs are saved as .skf format 2 - a big design saves in a fifth of a second instead of one to four, or at all:** The autosave writes a whole `.skf` package with the full undo history on every change, and format 1 put each body's CAD display edges into every body of every undo state. Measured on three edited bodies with 60 edges each across 50 undo states: 16 MB of `project.json`, 3.9 and 3.1 s for the first two saves and about 1 s for every one after; with 300 edges each the save failed outright at the 64 MB limit, so the design was not saved at all. Saving now writes format 2, the layout Formsmith746 writes since its commit e9cb8e8: an edge array is stored once as a file of its own and every body and state points to it, and meshes, edges, exact B-Rep text and images are encoded and hashed once per object and reused by the next save. The same design now saves in about 0.2 s with 103 KB of `project.json` (0.25 s with 300 edges each), and the files come out the same size as Formsmith746's to the kilobyte (2040 and 2057 KB). Format 1 files keep opening and turn into format 2 on their next save; a format 1 file written by the previous writer is kept as a test fixture for that. Formsmith746's reader opens a file written here, display edges shared, B-Rep, a rotated and cropped reference image and a construction line included. The price, decided knowingly: older versions of this fork and of Formsmith746, its released 1.0.9 included, do not open format 2 files.
- **Opens .skf files of format 2 from Formsmith746:** Formsmith746, the project this fork comes from, saves `.skf` format 2 since its commit e9cb8e8: the CAD display edges are stored once as a file of their own under `assets/display-edges/` instead of inside every body in every undo state, and those files demand a reader of version 2. Without this, a design saved there could not be opened here once that version is released. The reader now takes format 1 and 2, checks an edge file the way Formsmith746's own reader does (a list of edges, points in threes, all finite, no more than a mesh may carry), refuses one that is broken instead of half loading it, and gives every body and undo state pointing at the same edge file the same field, so they share it in memory as they do there. Saving was format 1 at this point; it is format 2 now, see the entry above. Tested against a file written by Formsmith746's own exporter, run from its current main: a box with two edges in seven undo states and a group whose child carries the same edges opens with all of them, the eight references share one field, and saving it back and opening that again keeps the edges. The other way round, Formsmith746's reader opens a file saved here, display edges, a rotated and cropped reference image and a construction line included - it keeps those fields although its own editor does not use them.
- **Push a flat face out or cut a pocket in:** A thicker wall, a raised pad or a pocket on a finished part took a workplane on the face, a traced sketch, an extrusion and a group - and on an imported STL there was no other way. A new tool in the Modify group, next to the hole pattern, does it in one step: click a flat face, choose **Out** or **Pocket**, and set the height or depth, an **inset from the edge** and a **corner radius**. The radius rounds every corner, the inner ones too, and the outline still ends exactly the inset inside the edge (it is offset in three steps: in by inset plus radius with sharp corners, then out and back in by the radius, rounded). Openings in the face grow by the inset. A pocket goes no deeper than the body behind the face. What it adds is an ordinary sketch body grouped with the body, so Edit group and Undo work as usual; the face is read from its triangles, the same way the hole pattern reads it, so it works on every flat face of any body, imported meshes included. The new part reaches 0.2 mm into the old one so the two fuse without a skin. Checked in the browser on a 40 x 20 x 10 box, top face, inset 3, radius 2, depth 4: pushed out, the group measures 40 x 20 x 14 and the downloaded STL 9890.07 mm3 in one shell, which is 8000 plus 4 x 472.52 - the 34 x 14 pad less its four rounded corners; as a pocket 6109.93, the same amount taken away. From layerling 1.80.0 (#404, contributed there by @mlugo-apx). Not taken over: layerling's special route for the top and bottom of a plain sketch body, which keeps the body's own exact curves; here a curved outline comes from the face's triangles, and the MCP action.
- **A blind hole or pocket is as deep as typed:** A hole is lengthened by 0.05 mm at both ends when it is grouped, so that no two faces lie exactly on each other - right for a hole that goes through, but a hole that stops inside the body came out 0.05 mm too deep. Measured in the browser on the hole pattern, 15 round holes of 4 mm typed 2 mm deep in a 40 x 20 x 10 box: 25.743 mm3 per hole, which is the 96-sided hole times 2.05 mm; now 25.115, times 2.000. A blind hole of the pattern, and the new face tool's pocket, are now shortened by that allowance; through holes keep it. Found while measuring the face tool.
- **The tube and the ring have an inner diameter:** Both had only the wall thickness, so a bushing for a shaft of a known size meant working out the wall by hand. The settings now have **Inner diameter** right under the wall: it is the width minus twice the wall, and typing it sets the wall. It is not a measure of its own - change the width afterwards and the wall stays while the inner diameter moves with it. The field shows what the body really has: where the stored wall is thicker than the body allows, the mesh caps it, and so does the number. It never leaves less than the 0.5 mm wall the wall slider allows, nor a closed tube. Checked in the browser: a new tube of 20 mm with a 4 mm wall shows 12; typing 8 makes the wall 6; setting the width to 30 then keeps the wall at 6 and shows 18. From layerling 1.77.0 (#384).
- **Arrow keys follow the snap grid, take fine steps without one, and Shift moves 5 mm:** An arrow key moved the selection by one millimetre whatever the grid was: on a 5 mm grid it took five presses to reach the next grid point, on a 0.25 mm grid a press jumped over three, and with the grid off, where the mouse drags freely, nothing finer than a millimetre was possible. The step is now the snap grid, and 0.01 mm with the grid off. Shift is the coarse step: 5 mm on every grid and without one, as it was before and as Formsmith746 has it - only on the 8 mm brick grid it stays at 8, so the coarse step is never smaller than the fine one. Checked in the browser before the Shift change: on the 1 mm grid one press moves 1 mm, and with the grid switched off three presses move from 1 to 1.03. The 5 mm with Shift is unit-tested for every grid, not clicked. From layerling 1.67.6 (#293) and 1.75.0 (#370).
- **A held arrow key saves once, when it is let go:** Saving the design after a move already waited 120 ms for the next one, but before a held key starts repeating there is about half a second, so a held arrow saved twice - after the first press and after letting go - and worked out the fingerprint of the whole design on every repeat. While an arrow key is down, saving now waits; letting go, or the window losing focus, saves once what came up meanwhile. Separate taps still save each, as they are separate moves. As in Formsmith746's commit e9cb8e8. Not checked in the browser: the development server had been stopped for lack of memory.
- **A run of arrow presses is one step in the history:** Every press of an arrow key that moved the selection (also Ctrl with Up and Down) was a step of its own: five presses to the right, and Undo took back one millimetre. Now a press that follows the last one within a second and a half, on the same selection and with nothing else done in between, replaces that step instead of adding one, so Undo takes back the whole run; a run that ends where it started leaves no step. One trap layerling's version of this falls into is avoided: when a run ends where it started its step is gone, and the newest step is the one before - creating the body, say. A press after that must not replace it, or Undo would skip the creation; the run starts afresh there. Checked in the browser: five presses right then one Undo goes from x = 5 back to 0; right, left, right then Undo goes to 0 and a second Undo takes the box away, and Redo twice brings it back at 1. From layerling 1.76.0 (#370). The sketch has no arrow-key moves, so this is the 3D editor only.
- **A loose hole is no longer lost from an exported body that touches another:** A hole left ungrouped is cut out of the bodies it reaches when exporting STL or OBJ. But bodies that touch or overlap are fused for the file afterwards, and that fusing rebuilt them from their shapes - without the holes cut into them a moment before. So a plate with a loose hole lost the hole as soon as anything stood on it. Measured in the browser: a plate 40 x 40 x 4 with a loose 10 mm hole exports 6.09 cm3; with a 10 mm cube standing on its corner the file had 7000 mm3 in 32 triangles - a full plate - instead of 6.69 cm3. The fusing now cuts the holes that reach the fused body into it again; the downloaded STL then measures 6686 mm3 in one shell, which is 7000 less the 314 mm3 of the hole. The material estimate in the export window went through the same code and showed the same wrong 7.00; it now shows 6.69. Found while looking at layerling's #352, not reported anywhere.
- **Fillet and chamfer on a body cut by a group of holes:** Two holes grouped first, that group made a hole and then grouped with a plate, ended in "The mesh is open or non-manifold" as soon as the edge tool opened, although the cut was perfectly closed; grouping the same holes directly with the plate worked. The edge tool took the hole group as one mesh of two loose pieces, which the kernel could not sew into one body. Two changes. The edge tool now opens a group inside a group when it holds nothing but holes, or nothing but solids, so every hole reaches the kernel as the exact shape it was - a group that mixes solids and holes cuts only its own solids and stays closed. And a mesh that sews into several closed shells now becomes one body: each shell is made a solid and they are fused, where before the first shell was all that counted and the result was refused. Checked in the browser, each with the previous code for comparison: the plate 60 x 40 x 6 with two 8 mm holes in a hole group now offers 18 edges, the same as with the holes grouped directly - so the hole rims are true circles, not facets - and a 1 mm fillet on its top edges rounds 6 of them (before: refused); an imported mesh of two separate 10 mm cubes now offers 24 edges and takes a fillet on its 8 vertical ones (before: refused). From layerling 1.79.0 (#394).
- **Top edges and vertical edges in the edge tool:** Next to **All sharp edges** the fillet and chamfer panels have two more quick picks. **Vertical edges** takes every edge that stands straight up - the corners of a box, the lines where the walls of an extruded outline meet - so one fillet rounds all the standing corners at once; an edge counts when every point of it stands over the same place within a thousandth of its height, so the slanted edges of a pyramid stay out. **Top edges** takes the edges that lie wholly at the very top of the body, such as the rim of a cookie cutter; an edge that only reaches up with one end, like a box's corner, stays out. Both pick only from the edges the tool offers at its threshold. Checked in the browser on a 20 x 10 x 5 box: 4 of 12 edges each, and the previews show the four standing corners rounded in one case and the rim of the lid in the other; All sharp edges still gives 12 of 12. The AI bridge takes `edgeIds: "top"` and `"vertical"` as well. From layerling 1.82.0 (#427) and, for the top edges, an earlier layerling release (#154).
- **A picture plate no longer slows down a drag:** The viewport decides whether a body's material must be rebuilt by comparing a signature, and that signature carried the picture plate's whole image data URL - several megabytes - and was built again on every sync, during a drag on every move. Measured with a 4 MB picture: 10.9 ms per body and sync, nearly a whole frame at 60 Hz. The picture is now recognised by its identity, which the signature already carried, plus the length of its data; nothing in the program changes a picture in place, so a new picture is always a new object. The same signature now costs 1.6 microseconds. From layerling 1.76.1 (#170). Measured on the signature alone, not as frame times while dragging in the browser.
- **The height above the plate is no longer held to 220 mm:** The number typed into the height-above-plate box at a body jumped back to 220 whatever was typed, the lift arrow stopped there, and - worse - a body that lay higher, placed there through the property panel, jumped back down to 220 the next time one of its handles resized it, because the resize kept its bottom in place and capped it on the way. 220 mm was the size of the old fixed plate. The limit is now 100 m up and down, as a guard against a typo such as 1e9 and not as a limit for a design. The position fields X, Y and Z in the property panel took any number upwards already, but cut a negative one at the slider's end and showed a body lying beyond it at that end; they now take any number either way and show it. Checked in the browser this time, through Chromium driven over the DevTools protocol: a box lifted to 30 mm, the lift handle clicked and 600 typed lands at 600 (the previous code, run the same way, at 220); X = -400 and Z = 500 typed into the panel are stored as such. From layerling 1.81.0 (#425). The sliders still reach as far as before; layerling also let them follow the printer's height, which is not in here.
- **Editing a sketch no longer moves the body:** Opening the sketch of an extruded body, pulling an edge outward and finishing centred the body on its new outline but kept its old middle, so everything you had not touched slid sideways by half of what the outline grew - and the sketch looked shifted when you opened it again. The body now moves with the middle of its outline: the shift is measured in the sketch, which is the body's own frame, and taken into the world with the body's rotation and mirroring, so a body on a standing face moves up or down where its sketch moved. Measured: a 20 mm rectangle pulled to 30 mm to the right moves a body at x = 100 to 105, so its left edge stays at 90; turned by 90 degrees about the vertical the same shift goes 5 mm backwards, mirrored it goes left, and tipped by 90 degrees about X a shift of 5 mm in the sketch's depth lowers it by 5 mm. Curves count with their bulge: a right edge bulged into a half circle of 5 mm puts the middle at 12.5 instead of 10. The outline's middle is taken from sampled curves, so a shallow arc lands a little short (10.4897 instead of 10.5 for a 1 mm bulge); the body itself is centred on its tessellated mesh, which has the same kind of error. Only for extruded bodies; a revolved or swept body has a different middle and still keeps its place. From layerling 1.70.0 (#291). Not clicked through in the sketch editor; the shift and its rotation are unit-tested.
- **The toolbar no longer flickers:** At some window widths the whole editor flickered between two sizes. It was the toolbar choosing its icon size: it measured itself in the size that was currently set and worked out from there how wide it would be in the full one, assuming that only the icons change. The smallest step changes more - sections lose their minimum width, the name field narrows, and the group boxes dissolve (`display: contents`) so their sections can wrap one by one. A dissolved box measures 0 px. Measured in Chromium at a 1400 px window: in full size the fixed sections came to 2056 px, in the smallest supposedly 208, so the full size seemed to fit, did not, and the bar switched back - every frame, about every 17 ms, 179 switches in three seconds, its height jumping between 185 and 261 px and the 3D view below resizing with it. The toolbar now lays out and measures each step itself, all within one pass so nothing is painted in between, and counts the sections of a dissolved box instead of the box. Since the answer no longer depends on the step currently set, switching to it gives the same answer again. Measured afterwards: no switch at all in three seconds at 1400, 1920 and 2600 px; one row measures 2276 px in full size, 2153 in the middle and 1846 in the smallest. The sketch-mode toolbar uses the same code but was not measured separately.
- **Crop the reference image:** The Align card has **Crop**: while it is on, the frame handles cut the picture down instead of scaling it, and a dashed frame shows how far the whole picture reaches. The picture keeps all its pixels - what is cut away is stored as a share of each side and comes back with **Whole picture**, at the same place and size. The point of doing it this way is the scale: cropping moves an edge of the visible part, not the picture, so a photo that was calibrated stays calibrated. Measured: pulling the right edge of a 40 mm picture to its middle leaves a 20 mm part whose centre moves 10 mm left, while the whole picture stays centred and 40 mm wide; cropping the top-left corner by a quarter and taking it back returns exactly the original frame; and on a picture turned by 30 degrees the whole picture stays put while the visible part moves its middle, and taking the crop back hits it again. An edge cannot be pulled beyond the whole picture or closer than 2 per cent to the opposite one, and a stored crop that would leave nothing visible is discarded on loading. Turning, cropping and calibrating fit together: the picture is drawn whole, clipped to the visible part, and turned about the visible middle. From layerling 1.61.0 (#216), the fourth item of that release. Not clicked; the crop arithmetic is unit-tested, the handles are not.
- **Reference image: rotate, centre, calibrate:** A photo laid under a sketch could only be moved and scaled - a template to trace, but not a measure. Its settings now have an **Align** card. **Rotation** turns it about its middle, so a part photographed at a slant stands straight to the revolve axis; the selection frame, its measures and its handles turn with it, and dragging a handle of a turned picture scales it in its own frame, with the opposite edge staying put as it does unturned. **Centre on X = 0** and **Y = 0** put its middle on an axis - X = 0 being the axis a revolve turns about. And **Calibrate**, as in Fusion: click two places in the picture whose distance you know, type the real distance, and the picture is scaled so that those two places are exactly that far apart. It scales uniformly about the **first** place, which stays where it is - that is the one you clicked first and lined up with - and it keeps the picture's proportions even if they were unlocked, because what is calibrated is the scale, not the shape. The calculation is measured: two places 12.5 mm apart in the picture and 50 mm in reality make it four times as large, the first place does not move, and the two places then lie 50.000 mm apart; the same holds for a picture turned by 33 degrees, because scaling uniformly about a point commutes with turning about the middle. The clicks for calibrating are taken from the raw pointer, not the snapped one - you point at something in the photo, and that rarely lies on the grid - and they are drawn as red rings with the measured stretch between them, above the picture. The rotation is saved with the picture. The fourth item from layerling's release, cropping the picture with its frame handles, is not in here. From layerling 1.61.0 (#216, asked for there by @ucito). Not clicked on screen yet; the rotation, centring and calibration arithmetic is unit-tested, the panel is not.

- **Ring gear:** The counterpart to the gear, and not just a gear turned inside out: what is a tooth on a gear is a gap here, so its teeth are pointed inwards and wide outwards, its tip circle lies **below** the pitch circle and its root circle above. And the centre distance is a difference instead of a sum - `module x (ring teeth - pinion teeth) / 2` - which is what makes a planetary set work out: ring, planets and sun, all of one module. It is set like the gear (module, teeth, pressure angle, backlash) plus the rim behind the tooth root, 1.5 module by default, because a thinner printed ring goes soft at the tooth row; the outside diameter is a slider of its own, since a ring usually has to fit a housing. The promise is measured the same way as the gears', with one trap avoided: the inner outline is a closed curve and "inside" it means **in the hole**, so a pinion point sits in the ring's material exactly when it is outside that curve - read the other way round, the measurement says the opposite. A 30-tooth ring and an 18-tooth pinion roll through a whole ring tooth pitch with no overlap and the flanks within 0.06 mm (the resolution of the two outlines, not a gap), and that holds for a 40-tooth ring with a 28-tooth pinion too. Turned the other way the pinion is immediately buried in the ring - internal gearing runs both parts the same way - and one millimetre further out there is no position without overlap. Two limits come with a ring, and the pressure-angle row names the span of pinions that fit between them: the pinion needs enough teeth not to be undercut (`2 / sin^2(pressure angle)`, the same number as the rack's), and the tooth numbers have to differ by at least 10, or the tooth tips butt into each other - measured on a 40-tooth ring, a difference of 10 is free, 8 digs in 0.013 mm and 6 digs in 0.219 mm; on a 60-tooth ring 8 is still free and 6 digs in 0.156 mm. - **Rack:** A new shape next to the gear, for turning a rotation into a straight line - a rack-and-pinion drive, a drawer, a linear axis. A rack is the gear with an infinitely large pitch circle, so its flanks are straight lines at exactly the pressure angle, and it is set like a gear: module and number of teeth, which give the length, plus pressure angle and backlash. The length is **exactly** a whole number of pitches (12 x 2 x pi = 75.398 mm by default), because the first tooth stands half a pitch from the end - two racks in a row therefore make one continuous row of teeth with nothing to work out. The depth takes the tooth (2.25 module) plus a back of one module, and nothing less. The promise is measured, not claimed, and it is a stronger one than the gears': a pinion of the same module is rolled along a whole tooth pitch in 120 steps - turned and moved by radius x angle, as rolling without slipping does - and at **every** position of that path there is no overlap and the flanks stay within 0.0097 mm (18 teeth) and 0.0101 mm (24), which is the outline's own resolution and not a gap. A drive that is only free in one position is no drive. One millimetre deeper there is no position without overlap, and a pinion with ten percent more module never fits. - **Gears with involute teeth that mesh:** New gears have involute teeth, set like a gear generator: number of teeth and module, which give the size (outside diameter module x (teeth + 2)), plus pressure angle (20 degrees by default) and backlash for printing (0.2 mm). Straight teeth are now called "Simple" and stay on older designs until the tooth shape is switched. The promise is measured, not claimed: two outlines of the same module, turned against each other in fine steps at the centre distance module x (teeth1 + teeth2) / 2, never overlap and their flanks lie on each other to within 0.015 mm - while one millimetre closer there is no angle without overlap. Checked from 6 to 30 teeth. An involute spur gear is also an exact body, bore included, so its tooth edges can be rounded - a root fillet is what keeps a printed tooth from breaking. One difference to the simple gear: its outline is no longer stretched to fill the bounding box, because that stretch would distort the module by up to 3.4 percent, so an involute gear is a few percent narrower than its nominal diameter - the outside diameter is the tip circle, and a tooth need not point along an axis.
- **SVG import keeps holes again:** A hole inside an outline - a round cut-out drawn in Inkscape, for one - came in filled, as a body of its own. Inkscape closes an outline of arcs about 0.0000002 mm short of its start, and the cleanup kept that sliver because its threshold was fixed at 1e-20; a segment of no length then counted every point as lying on it, so the hole "crossed" the outline. The threshold now scales with the drawing, as the point-in-ring test already did, and a zero-length segment holds only its own point. On the reporter's path the imported area was 4222 mm2 - more than the full 2827 mm2 disc, because the hole was counted on top of it - and is now 1709. From layerling, reported by @AkiraNorthstar (#197), whose path is the regression test.
- **Loose holes are cut on export:** A hole left ungrouped was dropped from an STL, OBJ or SVG export, though the STEP export already cut it - so one design gave a hole or no hole depending on the format, and the filament estimate counted without it. Now every visible hole that goes along is cut out of the bodies it reaches, as grouping would cut it; with a selection, only the selected holes count. Which holes reach a body is decided by their boxes, so a hole at the other end of the plate costs no boolean. If a cut fails, the status line says so and the file holds the bodies uncut rather than silently losing the hole.
- **A twist is drawn as a twist:** A box or a prism has nothing between its bottom and its top, so a twist only turned its lid: the side edges ran straight from one end to the other instead of following the turn, and the body was pinched in the middle - 4.14 mm on a 20 x 20 x 40 box twisted by 90 degrees, where the corner belongs at a radius of 14.14 mm and the straight edge passes at 10.00. The sides also folded along their diagonals, because a quad's two triangles no longer lie in one plane once it is twisted. The mesh is now cut into bands of 7.5 degrees of twist before it is deformed, which leaves less than 0.03 mm of that error, in the view and in the printed file alike. A cylinder was never affected (turning a circle about its own axis changes nothing), and taper, lowered sides and the top offset need no bands - they keep straight lines straight.
- **The height is called Z, as in the slicers:** The position card called the height Y and the depth Z, while the split tool already called the height Z - so our own two tools disagreed, and anyone holding our numbers next to Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, Tinkercad or a CAD program had two letters swapped. Y now runs towards the back and Z upwards, in the position card and in the pattern panel; the rows are ordered X, Y, Z. Designs do not move and nothing is stored differently - only the labels change. The mapping lives in one place now (`axisLabels.ts`), which the split tool reads too.
- **A straight knurl is an exact body:** Its grooves can now be rounded and chamfered, and it can be hollowed. The outline the kernel pulls up is the same ring of ridges and groove bottoms the mesh is drawn from, so the exact body is the drawn body - measured against the mesh's own volume at 30 and at 78 grooves. A 30-groove grip takes 36 ms to build and has 62 faces and 236 triangles; a 0.15 mm fillet on a groove edge takes 54 ms. The chamfer at the ends is cut as the mesh draws it, by meeting a 45-degree barrel, not by chamfering each rim edge - that would follow the groove flanks as well and leave a different body (4404 against 4418 mm3), and it is slower (8 s against 2.2 s at 78 grooves). With a chamfer the body takes about a second to build, which happens once when an edge tool starts. Crossed knurling stays a mesh: its grooves run on helices, and what two counter-turned rows have in common is not an extruded outline.
- **Align faces:** A new tool next to Lay flat brings a face of the selected part against a face of another part. Click the tool, the part's face, then the other face. "Face to face" puts them back to back, "Flush" lays them in one plane, and a gap leaves room between them (a negative one presses them together). The part turns the shortest way about the clicked point until the faces are parallel - not at all if they already are - and then slides only along the target face's normal, so it keeps its place sideways. A 30-degree-turned, 20-degree-tilted box lands with four corners exactly on the target plane and no point past it. From layerling, asked for by @RobbieKnobbie (#163).
- **Knurling:** A new shape for grips - a round body with grooves all around, for knobs, thumb wheels and tool handles. "Straight" runs the grooves along the axis, "Crossed" lays two slanted rows over each other into small diamonds. Diameter, height, number and depth of the grooves, the chamfer on both ends and, for crossed knurling, the grooves' angle can be set. No groove gets finer than 0.8 mm around the grip - finer than that no FDM printer shows - so the number of grooves is capped by the diameter: 78 on 20 mm, 23 on 6 mm. It sits right after the gear in the shape library. From layerling, which builds straight knurling as an exact CAD body through a profile extrusion we do not have; here it is a mesh, like every shape but the primitives.
- **A revolved sketch is an exact body:** Revolve now builds the body with OpenCascade instead of stacking slices into a mesh. So Hollow, Fillet and Chamfer work on it - hollowing a revolved cup failed before - and it has a few hundred triangles instead of tens of thousands. An arc in the outline stays an arc. Start angle and sweep work as before. A profile that crosses the axis, and the filled variant used for cutting, stay meshes; a body revolved by an older version stays a mesh too, and the edge tools now say so and name the way out (open Edit sketch and finish again). From layerling, reported by @ucito (#167).
- **Hollow can leave any side open:** Instead of top, bottom, both or none, there is a switch for each of the six sides, in any combination: front alone makes a slot for a drawer, left and right a tunnel. Two older faults came out while measuring this. With "bottom" open, hollowing used to fail with "no face found", and "top and bottom" opened only the top: the kernel already returns outward normals, and we turned the ones at the low end of each axis back inwards. And with no side open the body was not hollowed at all but shrunk by the wall thickness - a closed hollow body is now built as the outer body minus the inner one. The wall limit follows the openings: a measure only counts when both of its sides stay closed. From layerling, asked for by @RobbieKnobbie (#159).
- **Patterns can climb and spiral:** A row steps along X, Y and Z at once - a diagonal row, a staircase - instead of along one axis. A circle has a rise per copy (a screw) and a change of radius per copy (a flat spiral); both together give a conical spiral. A spiral running inward stops at the centre instead of throwing its copies through to the other side. From layerling, asked for by @mobiusfix (#145).
- **Small fillets no longer leave a sawtooth:** A fillet of about 0.2 to 0.9 mm round a large circular edge came out with visible zigzags along the seam, in the editor and in the slicer. The mesh strayed 0.1236 mm from the true surface at standard quality - five times the 0.025 mm the quality promises, because one triangle spanned most of the quarter arc. The mesh is now measured against its own faces and, where it strays, rebuilt with a tighter angle (0.1, then 0.05, under 400,000 triangles): 0.0084 mm at standard, 0.0021 mm at fine. Draft quality is left alone, because it keeps its own coarser promise. From layerling (1.45.0).

- **Split:** A new tool in the Modify group cuts the selection in two with a plane. It starts flat - the cut that brings a part too tall for the printer onto the plate - and can be set to any of the three axes, turned about the other two and moved along its normal; a translucent plane shows where it stands. "Pick a face" lays it on the next face you click, tilt included. Every selected body the plane crosses becomes two closed bodies, a hole becomes two holes, and a hollowed body keeps its cavity. The halves are meshes, so their shape settings are gone. From layerling, which took it from a SketchForge fork by WC3D.
- A group resized after grouping is exported, aligned and snapped at the size it shows. Its mesh put the parts together at the size they were grouped at, so STL, OBJ and 3MF got the old size and the filament estimate reported the wrong volume. From layerling, contributed by @gogades (#127).
- "Lay flat on face" lights up the flat face under the pointer before the click, so you can see which side goes down and turn the view to look at the others. On a mesh denser than 20,000 triangles only the facet under the pointer lights up: searching the whole face costs 22 ms at 24,000 triangles, and the click still searches it. From layerling, asked for by @Jeff-Haas (#130).
- A fingertip reaches 16 pixels beside the edge of a body instead of 6, because it covers more than a mouse pointer. The mouse is unchanged. From layerling, contributed by @rmpel (#123).
- Snap grids of your own: in the workspace settings, add a measure with a name and a size in millimetres - the 2.54 mm of perfboard, say - and the snap menu offers it whole, halved and quartered. A step keeps its millimetres, so a design still snaps right after its measure has been removed from the settings. From layerling, contributed by @rmpel (#118).

## 1.1.0

A minor release rather than a patch: everything below has been sitting
unreleased since 1.0.9, and it adds tools rather than only mending them.

New tools

- Hollow turns a solid into a shell with a chosen wall thickness, with any set of sides left open, built on the CAD kernel's `shell`/`offset` (occt-wasm raised to ^5.4.0 for the join type they need). The wall keeps its thickness when the body is resized afterwards instead of growing with it.
- Section view cuts the display along a plane so the inside is visible, without changing the bodies. What the plane hides cannot be clicked, measured, snapped to or pinned a note on; the cut face itself stays reachable.
- Point to point and point to workplane move the first-picked body so the two marked points coincide, or so the marked point lands on the active workplane.
- A target menu decides what a click grabs - free on a surface, free or centred on an edge, the centre of a face, or a corner - shared by point snapping, the rotation pivot and lay flat.
- Lay flat rotates a body so the clicked face rests on the workplane.
- The rotation pivot can be set by clicking a face; the body then turns around that point.
- Pattern repeats the selection in a row or around a circle.
- Cutting tools: trim flush against another body, trim at the workplane, and clear out the interior of a tube or any self-drawn hollow body.
- Three bore shapes join the palette as cutters: counterbore, countersink and teardrop hole.
- An object list shows every body, with range selection by Shift.
- A group can be opened to change its parts without taking it apart.
- Notes sit on the workplane or on a body and travel with the project; they stay out of every export.
- Region resize works on a box inside the object: only what the box holds is stretched, the rest keeps its shape.
- Loft morphs between two cross-sections.
- New shapes: rounded box, honeycomb, ellipse, polygon, thread, spring, ruler, loft, oval, semicircle, pie slice and bolt circle, plus threaded heads (lens head, set screw, Torx).
- Sketch mode gained real circles and arcs, rounded corners, alignment guides, a free rotation handle, a third mode where the shape follows a path, SVG import, and rotate/mirror/scale.
- Extrusion with twist and an offset top face; taper and height per side.

Print preparation

- Overhangs are hatched red and white in the view, from a chosen angle (30 to 70 degrees, 45 by default).
- A warning names the bodies that stand taller than the printer can build.
- The export window estimates volume, weight and filament length for PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, TPU or PA - worked out as solid, so it is an upper bound.
- 190 printers can be chosen by name; each sets plate width, depth and build height together. The numbers come from OrcaSlicer's profiles.
- The edge tool works on the exact body a STEP import brought with it instead of its triangles, so a round rim is one circle rather than dozens of facet edges.
- 3MF files can be read.
- STEP export carries a body whose edges were filleted or chamfered, rounding included.

Interface

- The toolbar measures itself and steps its icon size down, or wraps, instead of running off the edge - no more zooming the browser to 67 per cent to reach the last group.
- Point tools show a preview of what a click would grab before the click.
- A click on a corner handle types width and length together; Tab moves between them, Enter applies both in one step.
- Shift while dragging holds the body to one axis of the workplane; Alt while dragging leaves a copy behind.
- A box selection with Shift turns each body in it around, as a Shift click does for one.
- The snap grid stays reachable when the body settings are collapsed.
- The interface speaks German as well as English, down to the exercises, messages and shape names.
- Transparency per object, with a slider under the solid/hole choice.
- The arrow keys move a body the way the camera sees it.
- Axis chaining as an L/W/H switch in the toolbar.
- Frame the selection (Shift+F) and an orthographic view button in the camera bar.
- A saved project can be inserted into the open one, and every project can be backed up at once.

Fixes

- A saved project no longer opens into an empty workspace: the reference point was written into the file as a body kind the reader refused, and the failed read then replaced the stored project with nothing. The read now drops what it cannot use, keeps the file, and a failed read never overwrites anything.
- "This page couldn't load" is gone: the app has its own error page, which names the error and offers the way back instead of clearing the project.
- Clicking the gap between two parts of one object no longer selects the object, and neither does dragging a selection box through it. A press that just misses still means the body beside it.
- The reference point no longer ends up inside a group, no longer takes selection handles, and stays out of copies, exports and frame selection.
- Hollowing out an interior now also recognises a rotated tube, a baked body that was resized afterwards, and a self-drawn hollow body.
- Trimming flush now cuts against the body that was clicked last, not the one that was built last.
- The region box no longer slides through the body, keeps its taper, survives a rotation into the workplane, and stops exactly at its own faces.
- A rotated body remembers what it is and how it stands, so its rotation angle and its taper fields stay right.
- Undo brings the workplane back with it.
- Stored CAD geometry is no longer rebuilt as B-splines by a plain move - a fillet's analytic faces survive the next edit.
- Mesh density no longer falls back after later fillets, and fine edges stay clickable in the edge tool.
- Export merges overlapping bodies, so a file no longer holds two shells inside one another.
